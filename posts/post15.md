# Distance from fixes impacts value of same testing

Same tester, writing same test automation - yet the impact this tester makes on quality is entirely different. Sound like potential for framing the test automation investment in a way that gives up relevant parts of its value. So let me explain. 

A colleague has been offered three different client projects, and in all three she will work on test automation. This made it clear: the choice where the client(s) place her make a difference on the results the client will experience out of test automation. 

The three options are: 

1. Same team with developers

2. Testing services team

3. Client side team

Imagine you had full control over the choice of where you place your new pair of hands on testing / test automation, what is the impact of the setup? The bottom line: 

> If you are close to fixes, your testing does not just verify - it also impacts what flows forward in the overall efforts. 

In the same team with developers: 

- For timely feedback, timely fixes! Likelyhood of timely feedback is up by knowing exactly what change is going on right now. 
- Significant impact on quality: Fast fixing, test results guide development, quality improves continuously (making testing also easier)

In the testing services team: 

- The handoff frequency is by design, but often attention of tester is split to multiple teams
- Fixes find their way to the next iterations
- Medium impact on quality: Fixes with delay, test results with delay, slower improvement

In the client team: 

- Fixes made separately, and some feedback delayed to change framing for users and automation efficiency
- Inclined to prioritize for later, future
- Requires client reaction to improve quality, protects the knowledge client expects to have

For years, I have been pitching this: 

**When you invest in quality, push the investment into automation directly where the system is being built (or broken). On client side, ask for ability to run this test automation.**
